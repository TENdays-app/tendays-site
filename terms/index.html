What TENdays is — and isn’t

  • A planning tool, not an advisor. It organises what needs doing and offers guidance worth considering. It doesn’t know your field, your crowd or your council — you do. Nothing in the app is legal or professional advice, and using TENdays doesn’t guarantee a licence, a permission or an approval. Those decisions sit with your council and other authorities.
  • Its numbers are starting points. Ratios (first aiders, stewards, toilets), deadlines and fees are based on published guidance, which varies by authority and changes over time. Always confirm the real requirement with your council, your providers and your insurer — especially anything safety-critical.
  • You are the author of record. Documents the app produces are built from your answers and your confirmations. Read everything before you rely on it or submit it. If an answer going in is wrong, the document coming out will be too.
  • Common sense stays in charge. The app can contain mistakes. If something it says looks wrong for your event, trust your judgement and check with the real authority.

Using it fairly

TENdays exists to help committees run good events. So:

  • Use it only for lawful purposes and only for events you are genuinely helping to organise.
  • In chat, no harassment, threats, abuse, discrimination or unlawful content, and nothing that is illegal to share. Anyone can report a message from within the app, and block a person; reports reach the event’s leads and reach us at hello@tendays.app, which we read every day.
  • Only enter other people’s details — an invitee’s email, a volunteer’s name, the person named on a Temporary Event Notice — when you have a proper reason to and they would expect it. Our privacy policy explains how we look after that information.
  • Don’t try to get into events or accounts that aren’t yours, or to interfere with the service.
